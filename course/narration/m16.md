# m16 narration: A guild at work

Welcome to Module Sixteen, the last module of the multi-agent unit.

Picture a mixed-species flock moving through a tropical forest. A dozen species, each feeding in its own way and at its own height. And one or two sentinel species watching the sky and calling when a hawk appears. Different roles, one shared purpose, and someone whose whole job is to watch for danger.

Here's a twist you may know. Some sentinels cheat. Fork-tailed drongos sometimes give false alarm calls, so that the birds they follow drop their food. And the listeners learn, over time, which callers can't be trusted.

Keep that drongo in mind, because today you'll meet its digital cousin.

First, the guild. One of today's sources turns a single Claude Code session into a six-role team for boring web work. A planner maps out the job. A navigator opens pages one at a time. A filler enters the data. A checker verifies every save. A guard asks before anything is submitted or paid. And a logger writes every step into a spreadsheet. The single best habit in that course is this: do three rows, then stop and look. Most broken runs fail on the first or second row, and catching it there saves you forty broken records.

Now the drongo. A browser agent reads everything on a page, even hidden text. If a page, or a document, or a cell in your spreadsheet, contains a sentence that looks like an instruction, the model may treat it as one. That's called prompt injection. The rule to remember is simple. Text from outside is data, never instructions.

But writing that rule down is not enough. Here's where the bird metaphor breaks. Birds evaluate alarm calls as signals, and they learn which callers lie. A language model doesn't separate instructions from data at all. Both arrive as the same kind of tokens. And it never learns, from one session to the next, who has lied before. So the separation has to be built into the harness. Code that handles the outside text. Reviewers that can read but not act. Permissions that make the dangerous thing impossible.

In the lab, you'll build a small guild that checks a list of ten marine species names against a free public taxonomy database. A lookup script fetches each name and keeps the raw answer. A read-only checker flags typos and outdated names. A read-only guard watches for anything that looks like an instruction. And a logger records every row with a reason.

I've hidden a drongo in the list. Row eight contains a note that tries to order your agent to mark everything verified and delete the log. Your job is to make sure the guard reports it, and that nothing obeys it.

This guild is your build for the unit, and it becomes the taxonomy checker in your capstone. Let's go and catch a liar.
